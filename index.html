About

A writer who starts from the user's question

Everything I write is tested against one yardstick: can a reader follow this and finish the task without guessing?

I am a technical writer who specializes in software documentation for everyday users. My work centers on the documents that shape a product's first impression: getting-started material, in-product help, release notes, and the style rules that keep a documentation set consistent as it grows.

My samples on this site are written for products and workflows I know well — including browser-based software such as Microsoft Edge — because I believe portfolio writing should demonstrate the same research and empathy I bring to client work: knowing who the reader is, what they are trying to do, and where they are most likely to get stuck.

I write with a defined voice and a style baseline, illustrate procedures with screenshots, and verify instructions by walking through them step by step.

  • ✓ End-user procedures that match the real product flow — UI names, screenshots, and states included
  • ✓ Release notes that tell readers what changed and why it matters
  • ✓ Style guides that keep a growing docs team consistent
  • ✓ Plain, respectful language — no jargon, no filler, no "simply"

What I do

Documentation skills across the full product cycle

📘

User guides & procedures

Task-oriented instructions built around what the reader wants to do. Every step is written in the imperative, verified against the interface, and supported with annotated screenshots.

📣

Release notes

Release notes that organize change by reader impact — new features, improvements, bug fixes — written in plain language with version numbers, dates, and scoped sections for easy scanning.

📐

Style guides

Pragmatic editorial standards — voice, tone, terminology, formatting conventions — that keep one writer or a whole team producing consistent, on-brand documentation.

🛟

Troubleshooting & FAQs

Support content organized by symptom: likely causes, tested fixes, and the exact error messages and states a reader will see — so help arrives before the support ticket does.

🖼️

Screenshots & visual docs

Clear, current captures that sit beside the steps they illustrate — cropped, annotated, and captioned so images explain rather than decorate.

🔍

Editing & information architecture

Reviews for accuracy, consistency, and readability — plus the heading structures, cross-links, and page-level planning that make documentation findable and navigable.

How I work

From “what does this button do?” to shipped docs

1

Clarify the task

I start from the user's goal and work backwards — interviewing subject-matter experts and trying the product myself before writing a word.

2

Write to a standard

Imperative mood, active voice, consistent terminology, UI names formatted precisely. Every sentence earns its place.

3

Show, don't just tell

Screenshots are captured, cropped, and annotated to sit beside the step they support — never as decoration.

4

Verify & iterate

Instructions are walked through on the real product, reviewed for accuracy, and revised when a reviewer or reader gets stuck.
