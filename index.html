BS
Bengaluru, IN 5+ years Healthcare · Aviation

I am a technical writer with 5+ years of experience across two very different documentation worlds: regulated aviation maintenance manuals for GE Aviation and Boeing programmes, and healthcare software documentation at eClinicalWorks. Both taught me the same lesson from opposite directions — precision matters just as much in a user guide as it does in a component maintenance manual.

Today I author release notes, user manuals, FAQs, and troubleshooting content in DITA and DocBook XML, working inside the SDLC alongside engineers, SMEs, and QA. I led my team's migration from Adobe FrameMaker to Paligo, configuring PDF and HTML5 layouts, templates, variable sets, and reusable sections — and then training the writers who followed.

I hold a degree in Aeronautical Engineering, which is where the habit of reading a drawing before writing a sentence comes from. I'm a strong supporter of Docs as Code, and this portfolio — hand-coded HTML and CSS, published from a Git repository — is that belief in practice.

About the samples below: they are written for a familiar consumer product, Microsoft Edge, because client work is under NDA. They demonstrate the research, structure, and care I bring to every document — who the reader is, what they are trying to do, and where they are most likely to get stuck.

How I work

From “what does this button do?” to shipped docs.

01

Clarify the task

I start from the user's goal and work backwards — interviewing subject-matter experts and trying the product myself before writing a word.

02

Write to a standard

Imperative mood, active voice, consistent terminology, UI names formatted precisely. Every sentence earns its place.

03

Show, don't just tell

Screenshots are captured, cropped, and annotated to sit beside the step they support — never as decoration.

04

Verify & iterate

Instructions are walked through on the real product, reviewed for accuracy, and revised when a reviewer or reader gets stuck.
